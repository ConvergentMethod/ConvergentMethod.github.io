Welcome to the Convergence of Excellence.

Convergent Method was founded on a special vision: to unify multidisciplinary practices rarely housed within a single firm. By integrating the specialized expertise of general contractors, professional engineers, and healthcare providers, we deliver a cohesive, end-to-end delivery model tailored to complex facilities.

Our integrated approach guides clients through every phase of the project lifecycle: from initial concept and planning through construction execution and sustainable, long-term operations. This comprehensive oversight optimizes building performance, reduces lifecycle maintenance costs, and ensures safe, functional environments for staff, patients, and visitors alike.

At Convergent Method, our guiding operational standard remains simple: Know where you're going before you get there.

Marriott Marque Hotel

$180 Million 03

Fort Lauderdale Airport Rental Car Facility

This project constructed a nine-story, cast-in-place concrete structure spanning approximately ten acres per level, creating the largest combined-use facility of its kind for the public and 12 on-site rental car companies. Level one houses a vehicle service area equipped with state-of-the-art washing systems and a fueling operation with 126 dispensers and 150,000 gallons of storage capacity, while Levels 2 through 5 accommodate rental car operations. Because of the facility's unprecedented scale and scope, the project used specialized alternative compliance methods and enhanced life-safety safeguards to meet and exceed technical building code standards.

$170 Million 04

DFW Airport Terminal-A Parking Structure

This project demolished three existing Terminal A parking garage structures at DFW Airport and constructed a single new multi-level Enhanced Parking Structure (ESP). Built with cast-in-place, post-tensioned concrete and exterior perforated metal screen panels, the facility integrates pedestrian bridges, a parking guidance system, wayfinding signage, energy-efficient lighting, and dedicated electrical service upgrades. Executed on a fast-track schedule with both day and night shifts, the work required detailed coordination with DFW Airport and concurrent terminal construction operations.

$152 Million 05

Office Depot Global Headquarters

This LEED Gold-certified project constructed a five-story global headquarters with offices, executive suites, interview rooms, conference and presentation areas, and an exercise facility. The facility also included a non-fixed-seating auditorium and a 778-person courtyard. Beyond the main building, the $90 million scope encompassed two precast parking garages and surface parking for 2,100 vehicles, extensive landscaping and irrigation, and new storm, sanitary, fire, and domestic utility services.

$140 Million 06

Regional Medical Center & Mental Health Facility

This project constructed North Carolina's first combined prison and hospital facility in two phases, adding to an existing 1884 prison complex. Phase A included extensive site development, the demolition of ten buildings, underground utility installations, two guard towers, four parking lots, and a 15,000-square-foot central utility plant providing electrical, chilled water, steam, and fire protection services. Phase B added a five-story, 167,000-square-foot regional medical center (featuring a diagnostic clinic, labs, radiology, dental, dialysis, pharmacy, physical therapy, a 120-bed inpatient surgical unit, and four 30-bed secure medical housing pods) and a five-story, 149,000-square-foot mental health hospital, connected by corridors.

$130 Million 07

DFW Airport Terminal-A Phase 1 of 3

This project renovated one-third of a 40-year-old terminal at DFW Airport, beginning with the construction of a permanent security wall to isolate active airport operations and major building systems from the work zone. Before demolition commenced, thousands of existing overhead low-voltage wires were traced, tagged, and protected to prevent service disruptions. The comprehensive overhaul included structural alterations, baggage handling systems, architectural walls and finishes, and upgrades to mechanical, electrical, lighting, security, life safety, fire protection, and communication systems. Executed on a fast-track schedule with day and night shifts, the work required managing gate closures and close coordination with DFW Airport and American Airlines.

$77 Million 08

MD Anderson Zayed Lab Buildout

The interior scope featured Level 5 finishes throughout the corridors and public areas, paired with custom millwork for kitchens and beverage bars, each with a unique layout. Executed in a partially occupied building, the team coordinated all construction logistics through two service elevators.

$28 Million 09

DFW Airport Terminal-A Rail Station

This project constructed a new rail station at Terminal A serving as the DFW Airport terminus for the DART Irving-3 line. The facility features a main platform area, cast-in-place concrete retaining wall structures, $4 million in architectural canopies, glass-fiber-reinforced concrete (GFRC) panel fascia, and an elaborate lighting package. UTMB West Plant Hardening Phase 1

This project installed a flood barrier system to protect critical infrastructure up to an elevation of 20 feet Mean Sea Level (MSL), ensuring uninterrupted HVAC service across the entire UTMB campus during and after flooding events. $8 Million 12

DFW Airport Public Address/Voice Evacuation

This project extended the existing Terminal D public address (PA) system to Terminals A and C at DFW Airport, wiring existing speaker zones to new head-end equipment without disrupting airline operations. As part of a broader phased terminal renewal program, the upgrades established voice evacuation capabilities while replacing speakers and adding new speaker zones, amplifiers, and ambient noise sensors in unserved areas. Executed on a fast-track schedule with both day and night shifts, the work required detailed coordination with DFW Airport.

$750 Thousand 13

Dunstan Chiller Replacement

This project removed two existing chillers and replaced two air handling units (AHUs) within a fully operational 24 Hour Fitness facility. Using temporary ducting and phased shutdown sequencing, the new HVAC systems were integrated without requiring external temporary cooling equipment. The team coordinated all demolition and installation activities with property stakeholders and executed them during off-peak hours to prevent disruption to the active gym.
